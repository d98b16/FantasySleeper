Generated by `make_reports.py` from `data/backtest_results.csv`. Every number here is read from the results file, not typed.

## Protocol

Walk-forward. For each target season T in 2016-2025, train on every (N -> N+1) pair with N+1 < T, then predict T. No row from T or later ever enters training. Models are fit per position. Evaluation is restricted to players who had an ADP that season — the draftable universe, and the only population where the ADP baseline exists.

Two targets are scored separately, because rate and availability are different questions: **points per game**, and **season points** (rate x that method's own games estimate).

### Baselines

| baseline | definition |
|---|---|
| ADP | positional ADP rank mapped to points by isotonic regression fit on prior seasons only |
| last year's rate | next year's points per game = this year's |
| positional average | the position's mean in the training data |

The ADP baseline is fit on **positional** rank, not overall rank. An overall-rank fit is a strawman that hands the model a fake win: a QB at overall pick 80 gets assigned the average points of the RB/WRs drafted near him, so ADP 'loses' on QB by 58% MAE while still having the better rank correlation. That version was caught and discarded.

## Verdict

Best method by MAE at each position — QB: ADP + ridge residual; RB: ADP (the market); WR: ADP + ridge residual; TE: ADP (the market).

**ADP wins everywhere.** A model trained on 13 seasons of opportunity data does not beat a public consensus board, on either target, at any position. That is the headline result and it is reported as such.

The reason is not that the features are bad — they are the right features, and the stickiness table shows they carry real year-over-year signal. It is that ADP is set days before the season and already prices in everything the model can see plus everything it cannot: coaching changes, holdouts, camp reports, depth-chart moves, and offseason trades. The model is fighting the market with strictly less information.

information makes things worse.
